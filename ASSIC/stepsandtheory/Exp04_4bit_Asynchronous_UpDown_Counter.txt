================================================================================
EXPERIMENT 4: Design, Synthesis and Simulation of 4-bit Asynchronous Up/Down Counter
================================================================================

AIM:
Design, simulate and synthesize a 4-bit asynchronous (ripple) up/down counter with 
enable and asynchronous reset. Verify functional behaviour for up and down counting, 
observe ripple propagation (timing), and discuss synthesis limitations and advantages.

THEORY:
1. Asynchronous (Ripple) Counter:
   - Only the LSB flip-flop is clocked by the external master clock signal.
   - Each subsequent higher-order bit is clocked by the output (Q or ~Q) of the preceding stage.
   - Up Counting: Next stage clocked on falling edge of Q (or rising edge of ~Q).
   - Down Counting: Next stage clocked on rising edge of Q (or falling edge of ~Q).
2. Signals:
   - clk       : Master input clock (drives bit 0).
   - reset     : Asynchronous master reset (clears counter to 4'b0000).
   - up_down   : Direction control (1 = Count UP, 0 = Count DOWN).
   - count[3:0]: 4-bit register holding current count value (0 to 15).

ADVANTAGES & LIMITATIONS IN ASIC DESIGN:
- Advantage: Extremely low hardware overhead, no complex next-state decoding logic.
- Limitation: Cumulative ripple delay (t_delay = 4 * t_pd_FF), causing intermediate glitches 
  during state transitions. In modern synchronous ASIC flows, synchronous counters with global 
  clock distribution are preferred for STA (Static Timing Analysis) closure.
